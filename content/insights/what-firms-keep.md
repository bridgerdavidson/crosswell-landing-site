Picture the Friday your best analyst gives notice. Somewhere in a personal chat account sits a year of prompts, corrections, and context: how your firm reads a deal, what it flags, what it ignores. On Monday, that account walks out with them. Agentic AI, meaning AI that does the work rather than just answering questions, produces two things every time it runs: the output, and a residue of context about how your firm works. Most firms measure the first and let the second evaporate. That gap, not the hours saved, is what this technology will end up being judged on.

## How fast is this actually moving?

In Gallup's latest workplace survey, 50 percent of U.S. employees said they use AI in their role at least a few times a year, up from 21 percent in 2023, with 13 percent using it daily ([Gallup, 2026](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/704225/rising-adoption-spurs-workforce-changes.aspx)). Stricter definitions produce smaller numbers, and the honest range matters: Pew Research puts workers whose work is actually done with AI at about 21 percent ([Pew, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/10/06/about-1-in-5-us-workers-now-use-ai-in-their-job-up-since-last-year/)). But the trajectory is not in dispute. In what they describe as the first nationally representative U.S. survey of the technology, economists Alexander Bick, Adam Blandin, and David Deming found generative AI being adopted faster than either the personal computer or the internet at the same point in their history ([Management Science, 2025](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32966)).

So the question facing a lean firm is no longer whether this arrives. It is what to measure once it does.

## Why is hours saved the wrong scoreboard?

Every vendor leads with time, and the time is real: professionals surveyed by Thomson Reuters expect AI to free up around five hours per week within the year ([Thomson Reuters, 2025](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute/reports/future-of-professionals-2025)).

Here is the distinction that matters more. Hours saved are a flow benefit. They arrive this week, get absorbed into this week's work, and reset to zero on Monday. Two firms with the same models will not get the same value from them, because workflow design, judgment about where to apply the tools, and follow-through all differ. But even the firm that wins on efficiency is winning a race that restarts every week.

Captured context is different in kind. It is a stock. The memo the system drafted is a flow; the record of why the firm framed it that way is a stock. Each meeting filed, each decision recorded with its reasoning, each document read into a shared memory adds to an asset that is still there next quarter and next year. Efficiency is worth buying. An asset that compounds is worth building. Most firms are currently buying the first and not building the second, because the first shows up on a stopwatch and the second does not.

## What walks out the door when someone leaves?

Today, at most firms, each person runs their own chat tool, and every conversation lives with that one person. A year of teaching it how the firm thinks, then a resignation letter, and all of it leaves in a personal login. The old knowledge-loss problem, concentrated.

The numbers on that old problem are brutal. In APQC's 2025 study of knowledge loss, 92 percent of organizations said they do not consistently capture knowledge from soon-to-be retirees, and over half of the entire workforce is planning to retire in the next five years ([APQC, October 2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/apqc-study-warns-looming-great-130000249.html)).

An honest limit first: no system keeps everything. Judgment, instincts, and relationships leave with the person, always have, and no software changes that. What a firm-owned memory keeps is the written trail: the analyses, the decisions and their stated reasons, the client history, the answers to questions the next person will otherwise ask one interview at a time. Gallup puts the cost of replacing an employee at one-half to two times their salary, and calls that a conservative estimate ([Gallup, 2019](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/247391/fixable-problem-costs-businesses-trillion.aspx)). Its own experience is that it takes 12 months or more for most people to get up to speed in most jobs ([Gallup, 2019](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/246242/essential-ingredients-effective-onboarding-program.aspx)). A shared memory does not repeal those numbers. It attacks the specific part of them spent reconstructing context that already existed somewhere in the firm.

That reconstruction cost is not hypothetical. In a 2025 survey of 4,000 employees by the enterprise search company Coveo, people reported spending around three hours a day just looking for information ([Coveo, 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/coveo-ex-relevance-report-reveals-42-of-information-fails-employees-too-much-data-not-enough-relevance-302439738.html)). A firm that cannot find what it already knows pays for the same knowledge twice.

The design decision, then, is not which model to pick. It is where the residue lands: in personal accounts that leave, or in a memory the firm owns.

## What should you ask instead?

Not "how many hours would we save." Ask this: if our best person gave notice tomorrow, what do they know that we have nowhere else?

## Frequently asked questions

### What is agentic AI, in plain terms?

AI that does the work, not just answers questions. It drafts the report, files the meeting, chases the missing document, and assembles the briefing, with a person reviewing what it produces. Every one of those actions also generates context, and where that context is stored is the decision this article is about.

### How long does it take a new hire to become productive?

Gallup's own experience is 12 months or more for most people in most jobs. A firm memory a new hire can question directly compresses the part of that year spent hunting for context: they inherit the written record on day one instead of rebuilding it one conversation at a time.

### Will AI replace financial advisors and fund professionals?

The evidence so far says it replaces tasks, not judgment or relationships. The nearer-term risk for a lean firm is quieter: the tasks move to AI, and the context those tasks generate accrues to personal accounts instead of to the firm.
